# #189 scientific-semantic erratum — 2026-09-28

This versioned erratum corrects two interpretations in the #189 schema-2
capability map. It does not alter the sealed capture, raw trajectories, frozen
protocol, case commands, thresholds, or nine-attempt budget. The original
`RESULTS.md` remains the capture record; this file supersedes only the specific
semantic statements below.

## Conclusions retained

- The 5 cm step, 10 cm step, and repeated 5/15/5 cm terrain cases remain
  **PASS** under their frozen route and task-goal checks.
- The flat reference and the half-speed, reverse, lateral, and yaw case
  classifications remain unchanged. In particular, the yaw probe remains

- These results remain bounded to the captured checkpoint, adapter, reset,

## `low_friction_cross`: geometric pass, no low-friction robustness evidence

The original **PASS** records that the robot met the frozen tracking,
cross-axis, route, and task-goal checks in `scene_low_friction_patch.xml`. It is
not evidence of low-friction robustness.

In the compiled v1 scene, the foot collision geoms have priority 1 and the
patch has priority 0. MuJoCo therefore selects the foot friction for
foot–patch contacts. The patch also overlaps the infinite normal floor, so a
foot in the patch region can contact both surfaces. A zero-step runtime audit
confirmed all four representative feet had both contacts and that each
foot–patch `mjContact` used friction `[0.8, 0.8, 0.02, 0.01, 0.01]` with
`dim=6`, matching the foot rather than the intended patch friction. The patch
geom's own friction values do not establish the effective contact parameters.

Accordingly, retain the old geometric/task-goal classification but withdraw
any interpretation of it as a low-friction capability result. This defect
does not invalidate the 5 cm, 10 cm, or repeated-step conclusions.

The v1 scene is preserved unchanged. The versioned successor
[`scene_low_friction_patch_v2.xml`](../../../unitree_robots/go2/scene_low_friction_patch_v2.xml)
uses finite normal-floor segments outside the patch and gives the patch higher
contact priority. A runtime `mj_forward` regression checks actual supporting
contacts, effective friction, and contact dimension. No capability experiment
was run on v2.

## Corrected yaw recomputation from the sealed trajectory

MuJoCo free-joint angular velocity is already expressed in body coordinates.
The schema-2 analyzer and its independent verifier incorrectly applied a
second quaternion rotation to `qvel[3:6]`. The corrected yaw metric uses the
logged body-local `qvel[5]` directly; the linear velocity projection is
unchanged.

The existing #189 `flat_yaw_probe` raw trace has SHA-256
`4b87f690d9c2e1a88e1dd99f3234574b7adc40b83c1700a3517e50bebe479697` and was
unchanged before and after recomputation. For frozen command `wz=0.5 rad/s`,
the corrected 2,400-sample window has mean body-local `qvel-z` **0.1038956543
rad/s** and MAE **0.3961043457 rad/s**. The unchanged frozen tolerance is
**0.1 rad/s**, so the case remains **PERFORMANCE_FAIL**. This is an offline
reanalysis of the sealed trajectory with `physics_steps=0`, not a new
experiment.

The schema-2 implementation, independent verifier, and permanent regression
tests now share this semantic contract. The #194 evidence package records the
source trace hash, selected window, corrected metrics, and zero-physics
recomputation.
